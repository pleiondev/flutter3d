# The product review of the 1.0 target state (2026-10-09)

The readiness review (`1.0-readiness-review.md`) asked whether the tree is
ready; this review asks whether **the target state** is ready to be sold
as a product. The target state is what §3 and the 58 decisions of §6 of
the readiness review describe, ordered by `1.0-rc1-plan.md` into rc.1
and rc.2. Five read-only audits, one per product dimension, fed it; their
full notes are `1.0-audits/20-…` to `24-…`. Dmitrii's four answers of the
same evening (§6) are taken as given.

## 1. Verdict

The target state is a sound engineering target and not yet a product
offer. Four things stand between the two.

1. **Three audiences are declared; the evidence supports one.** Games
   have artefacts anyone can touch (five browser demos, four genre
   packages, 96 goldens) and the only dated proof gate (decision 51).
   Twins have no product in the repository by the owner's own decision
   of 2026-09-15, and rc.1 gives them hooks nobody has written against.
   Laboratories have the most complete plan of the two non-game
   audiences, and that plan is cut in half: the teacher writes an
   experiment in rc.1 and a student sees it in rc.2. No public page
   names twins at all; education has one page with no link from the
   index. (`20-audiences.md` §0, §5.)
2. **The 1.0 label promises more than the repository can hold.** Of
   thirteen promises the label carries, the tree keeps five by itself
   (semver mechanics, deprecation rules, formats, the `flutter_gpu`
   isolation, publishing); five depend on hardware and people outside
   the tree (a Mac runner, an A55, an iPhone, eight phones, a signing
   certificate); three are not kept by design (empty types under semver,
   Impeller memory through the collector, package counts that disagree).
   Of fifteen tag conditions, nine have no threshold. (`22-release-definition.md` §1, §2.)
3. **The plan is a quarter, not a sprint.** rc.1 is about 190 agent-days
   on a critical path of about 13 weeks through nine sequential waves;
   rc.2 adds about 92 and 6. The most underestimated line is step 2.2:
   twelve decisions, one agent, four backends. (`22-release-definition.md` §3.)
4. **Adoption is the chosen measure and no plan item moves it.** The
   repository has 32 stars, one contributor, two open issues both
   unanswered, Discussions off, no chat, no release page, a site two
   versions behind, deployed by hand. The plan's only outward action is
   "videos and posts". (`24-ecosystem-ops.md` §3, §7.)

None of this is an argument against the engine. It is an argument that
the release has to be defined by what can be proven on the tag date, and
that the work that moves adoption has to be written down beside the work
that moves the engine.

### 2.1 Where the decisions paper over a conflict

- **Verifiability against accuracy.** The education page and the vision
  promise that a server checks a student's run by replaying it. Replay
  across machines needs bit-exact determinism. Decision 3 tells labs to
  pick `accurate`, which has "no cross-platform bit promise", and
  decision 33 says `accurate` "ships poses, not a resimulation". The
  lab that must be verifiable stays on `deterministic`; no decision says
  so. (`20-audiences.md` §4, `23-twins-labs.md` §4.)
- **`accurate` is the Dart engine.** The C core stays `float`
  (plan 3B). The Dart engine has no rigid-body rotation and no triangle
  meshes; fire, heat, water and wind live in the core. So the "accurate"
  laboratory of 1.0 is point masses, liquids and cloth. Nowhere is a
  user told. (`23-twins-labs.md` §4.)
- **A float world for buildings and sites.** Origin shift at ~1 km with
  a 5 km view test, physics tested at 10–90 m, a prose paragraph on
  precision and no number. Fine for a device twin; unstated for a site.
- **`externalIds` is labelled "for twins" and sits on substances**, not
  on scene elements; the BIM twin-ID mapping a buyer needs comes only
  from USD custom attributes. (`20-audiences.md` §2.2.)
- **Telemetry at 1 Hz inside a 60 Hz step** is one sentence
  ("assimilation inside the step decides"); no interpolation, no
  buffering, no "what to show while data is late". (`20-audiences.md` §4.)

## 3. The adoption path

The first hour, as the plan writes it, for a Flutter developer on macOS
who wants to show a GLB (`21-adoption.md` §7, twenty steps): eight exist
today, eight close in rc.1, two only in rc.2, four are not planned. The
four unplanned are `flutter clean` after adding the build dependency,
what happens after `flutter upgrade` (a stale bundle or a `flutter_gpu`
break, then a CPU fallback), hot reload of `onCreated`, and where to ask.
The two rc.2 steps are the ones the audience comes for: a GLB from a CDN
and a poster, hotspots and an AR button.

Other findings of the same audit:

- **"Two commands and forty lines" holds in rc.1 for the sphere from the
  template**, not for a viewer. The viewer template of 5.6 will be
  written on an API without `ModelViewer3D` and rewritten in rc.2.
- **`flutter pub add flutter3d` brings 0.8.5**, because `^1.0.0` does not
  admit a pre-release; neither quickstart nor README writes the
  constraint. (`21-adoption.md` §1.2.)
- **Item 5.4 is a week for one agent** and holds about twenty documents,
  `doctor` code with tests, and twenty compilable recipes, three of
  which depend on rc.2 API. The cookbook needs its own item with its own
  acceptance (files in `examples/cookbook/`, built in CI).
- **The versions promise is strong as tooling and weak as a promise**:
  LTS "None is named yet", "a patch does not change a frame" checked by
  hand on one Mac, `flutter_gpu` as `sdk: flutter` breaking with each
  stable, and "one number" against "a patch of `flutter3d_impeller`
  only" unresolved (SUPPORT:162 against :218). (`21-adoption.md` §3.)
- **Migration from 0.8**: 1 538 table entries, 721 by hand (47 %),
  README says "about two hundred places"; the guide describes a
  `GameLoop` deprecation that decision 14 forbids; the tool's own tests
  are red until wave 1; 0.6 and 0.7 have no path. 3/5 for 0.8, 1/5
  before. (`21-adoption.md` §4.)
- **The editor**: by URL is the real advantage and nearly free; the
  native builds of rc.1 ship Windows before anyone has opened it there,
  Linux drawing through the CPU rasteriser until 4C, and macOS behind
  one person's certificate with no unsigned fallback named.
  `gallery.md:54` denies a web build the editor README describes.
  Decision 32 sits in step 4.1 (rc.1) and in the rc.2 list at once.

## 4. The release definition

### 4.1 Promises against the tree

`22-release-definition.md` §1 lists the thirteen promises. The ones the
market will test first: rollback between devices (the C core's bitwise
parity across clang/ARM64, MSVC and Wasm is not shown; `doc/parity/`
does not exist; `digest_platforms.sh` knows no wasm), WebGPU as the
browser default (its goldens were never re-recorded and its suite
declines in CI), Windows and Linux as Supported after one session (the
file's own definition of Supported needs "a person with a device"), and
LTS for 18 months with one maintainer and no release named.

### 4.2 Gating

Of fifteen conditions, nine have no threshold: median, p99 and RSS of the
pacing report, the soak, what to do when parity fails, rollback, build
sizes, the nightly p99 number, the Windows session, the laboratory
tolerance, the allocation margin. Of the five hardware proofs, two have
no tool (RSS and soak; resimulation time per step, the session exposes a
flag only), one has no bench (eight phones) and one no target file.
The only inherited threshold, "a frame over 50 ms is a spike", comes from
a macOS job marked `continue-on-error`. (`22-release-definition.md` §2.)

### 4.3 Schedule

| | S | M | L | Agent-days | Critical path |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rc.1 | 7 | 26 | 21 | ≈ 190 | ≈ 66 working days |
| rc.2 | 2 | 10 | 12 | ≈ 92 | ≈ 28 working days |

With S = 1, M = 3, L = 5 days, nine sequential barriers, and every size
true. Step 2.2 (twelve decisions, one agent) is "L+" and is not an
estimate. Steps 3.0 → 3.1 → 3.2 are fifteen sequential days for the
catalog. Wave 5 is one person and his hardware. (`22-release-definition.md` §3.)

### 4.4 The freeze risk

The plan lands types in rc.1 "as a contract with no implementation
behind it": `XrSession`, `Rule`, `f3d.experiment`, `bindings:`,
`f3d.pipeline`, the Wasm render ABI, `PropertyLaw`. `1.0-stability.md:57`
says "anything not ready is finished or made private before 1.0.0". A
public empty base under semver without `@experimental` is the
contradiction. The first real WebXR session, the first teacher editing
an experiment, the first 200 catalog entries under a law type will each
want a different shape, and a shape change is a major. The repository
already allows the escape: packages on their own 0.x line
(`SUPPORT.md:179–181`). (`22-release-definition.md` §4.)

### 4.5 Platforms, as the announcement can state them

macOS Metal: Supported, checked nightly on the author's machine when it
is on, once the nightly exists. Android: Supported on the Galaxy A55
(Vulkan); not "Android". iOS: Best effort until a named device has a
report in `doc/pacing/`. Windows: Best effort after one session; not
Supported. Linux Impeller: CI under Xvfb means software GL. Web WebGL2:
CI; the wasm build of the demos is off (README:184 says otherwise).
Web WebGPU: Supported only after re-recorded goldens and a GPU Chrome in
the nightly. (`22-release-definition.md` §5.)

### 4.6 Legal and operational gaps of a 1.0 library

SECURITY says "nothing published" with 42 packages on pub.dev and names
a threat model without the relay, LTI and MCP; the root `LICENSES.md`
README links to does not exist; the LTC licence file is not at the path
`ltc.dart` names; the SMAA shaders carry no notice; no line says the
project is not affiliated with Google or Flutter; ROADMAP speaks of
0.7.0 and "not doing comparison tables". (`22-release-definition.md` §6.)

## 6. Against Dmitrii's four answers

1. **All three audiences, equally.** Then each audience needs a proof
   condition on the tag, not only games (§8.2), and the site needs the
   three-audience sentence that today exists only in `tasks/`. Without
   that, the announcement claims twins and labs and the site confirms
   games. The honest form of "equally" at 1.0: one engine, three
   examples (game, twin-shaped, laboratory-shaped), one proof each.
2. **pleion.dev services; the reference twin is an internal project.**
   This makes decision 4 coherent: the engine sells "the 3D and
   simulation layer for your twin", and pleion.dev sells the layer
   above. The announcement must say exactly that, and the dangling
   `flutter3d_lab_twin` references must go or point at the example.
3. **No pilot.** Every claim must be provable from the repository on the
   tag date. "Nobody outside has run it" stays true for the C core, the
   rollback, the twin hooks and the experiment format; the announcement
   says what is tested, not what is used.
4. **Adoption as the measure of ninety days.** Then the first hour, the
   two unanswered issues, a channel, a deployed site, a release page and
   a launch checklist are release work, as much as the hot path. None
   of them is a plan item today; §8.3 adds them.

## 8. Recommendations

### 8.1 Define 1.0.0 by what can be proven

**1.0.0 = rc.1 minus the research contracts, plus thresholds, plus two
weeks without a break.** Concretely (`22-release-definition.md` §8):

- In: waves 0 and 1 whole; wave 2 (A1–A10, D8, D9, D11–D13, D25, D36,
  D39, D41, D42, D46, `TimeSource`); from step 2.2 only what guards
  against a major in the HAL and the shaders (D24 AST as `abstract base`
  with a visitor, D25 encoder bodies, D26 the compiler registry, D27 the
  shader contract); from 4.1 the shader selection (D15/D19), the
  reference backend, 4B, 4F and the quickstart work; wave 5 with the
  thresholds below.
- Out, as minors 1.1–1.3 (all additive): the rest of 2.2, waves 3.0–3.2
  (the catalog stays at its 33 sourced entries; `PropertyLaw` arrives as
  a new type beside the number, not instead of it), 4D, 4E, 4H, 4I,
  4K', step 4.2 and wave 4.5. `SimulationProfile` stays as the marker
  type because it changes `SimulationVersion`.
- **`flutter3d_preview` on a 0.x line**, published with 1.0.0, holds
  `XrSession`, `Rule`, `f3d.experiment`, `bindings:`, `f3d.pipeline`, the
  Wasm render ABI and `PropertyLaw`. Moving a type into a 1.x package is
  a minor there and a major in 0.x: the break happens where it is legal.
- **Not said in 1.0.0**: LTS, "rollback between devices" before P3,
  "WebGPU default" before a GPU Chrome nightly, Windows or Linux
  "Supported".
- **Numbers**: rc.1 after the trimmed waves with P1, P3, P5 and seven
  green nights; two weeks of feedback with no `Breaking` line; 1.0.0 =
  rc.1 plus patches, or rc.2 and two more weeks if a break was needed.

Thresholds to write into the plan beside the five hardware proofs and
into `tool/pacing.sh` as `--fail-on`:

### 8.2 One proof condition per audience on the tag

Because the launch leads with all three (§6.1):

- **Games**: P1, P3, P5 with the thresholds above. Already decision 51.
- **Twins**: the twin-shaped example (4K) reads one real source, not a
  sampler: a published `MqttExternalStateInput` on `mqtt_client` or a
  REST poller, in the example's own code, with the history tape and a
  scrub through `RunTimeline`. The five `flutter3d_lab_twin` references
  are removed or pointed at it. `GeoOrigin` (lat, lon, alt, ENU) as a
  record on `Level`, named in decision 4 and absent from the table. The
  precision sentence in CONTRACTS carries a number ("± X mm at Y km
  from the site origin, physics in float32 relative to the shifted
  origin") and a physics test at 5 km, not only a view test.
  (`23-twins-labs.md` §6.)
- **Laboratories**: a named analytic test with a number
  (`pendulum_period_test.dart`: period at θ₀ = 0.1 rad over 100 periods
  within 1e-4 of the series, with an energy-drift bound) and the number
  on the education page; the `check → AgsClient/XapiClient` wire moved
  from 5F (rc.2) to rc.1, since the clients and the service exist;
  `Semantics` and `textScaler` in the lesson viewer and the lab panel
  moved from 4J to step 4.1; a `pendulum.f3d.experiment` fixture read by
  a test before the editor edits it; one Chromebook and one iPad in
  `doc/pacing/`. And the sentence decision 3 lacks: a verifiable lab
  stays on `deterministic`; `accurate` is for demonstration.
  (`23-twins-labs.md` §6.)
