# 0.10 plan

Drawn from `vision.md`: the first engine on Flutter for 3D games, industry
and education.

Decided with Dmitrii on 2026-10-08. There are five streams, each with its own
agents, running in parallel:

- A: the renderer, thirty items.
- B: what the simulation alone makes possible, thirty items.
- C: the fifth genre (`0.10-explore-genre.md`).
- D: what games need from an engine, twelve items.
- E: visualising for teaching, science and industry, fifteen items.

Each item is
done when it has a test that fails without it, a changelog line, and a
number on the A55 wherever it is about speed.

## Stream A: the renderer on phones and the web

### A1. Speed on mobile GPUs

1. **Materials at mediump.** Material shaders default to mediump, with
   explicit highp on positions, texture coordinates, depth and the HDR
   accumulators. Write down the precision contract (what must be highp
   and why) next to the shaders. The noise and anything with a Dart twin
   stay highp, because their parity is a float32 contract. Measure the
   frame time on the A55 and in a browser before and after. The goldens
   are re-recorded only where a difference is explained.
2. **No `discard` on the opaque path.** The opaque variant of the lit
   shader has no reachable `discard`, so early depth and hidden-surface
   removal stay on. Alpha-masked draws are cut through a depth pre-draw.
3. **A smooth LOD cross-fade.** Opaque LODs cross-fade through the same
   pre-draw, translucent ones by opacity. Today an LOD switch is a pop.
4. **Frames in flight.** Make the hard-coded three configurable. When the
   GPU is behind, the view presents its previous image instead of
   stalling the UI thread, and the held frames are counted.
5. **A frame-rate cap.** Rendering is held to an even cadence, for example
   every other refresh on a 120 Hz screen.
6. **Warm-up in slices.** `Renderer.warmUp` takes a time budget a slice
   and yields between slices, so input is answered during warm-up.
7. **Pipeline stalls named.** A pipeline build of 8 ms or more is reported
   with the material and geometry that triggered it. It also goes to the
   frame callbacks.

### A2. Depth for large worlds

8. **Reversed float depth** where the backend supports it (Metal, Vulkan,
   WebGPU, WebGL2 with clip control). Projections and picking keep the
   authored planes.
9. **The near plane fitted** to what is visible, every frame.
10. **An infinite far plane** for perspective projections.
11. **Depth layers.** Surfaces sharing a plane (road paint, signs, decals
    on walls) are ordered by layer at any distance. Diagnostics come with
    them: pairs of nodes that trade pixels as the camera moves, coplanar
    overlaps found in a scene, and a debug view of the gap two surfaces
    need.

### A3. The material language (`.f3dmat`)

12. **A `vertex {}` block** with the model transform. It runs in the depth
    and shadow passes too, so geometry moved there casts the shadow it
    draws.
13. **`ambient {}` and `composite {}` hooks**, and switches that compile
    out the environment light and the directional light.
14. **State in the file.** Blending (with additive), depth write and
    test, alpha-to-coverage, the depth layer, and putting a translucent
    surface into the depth the effects read.
15. **Scene depth and world position** readable from a material, for soft
    edges, fog and water in user materials.

### A4. The web and assets

16. **Images decoded by the browser** with mip chains built on the GPU.
    Today WebGL builds no mips.
17. **A size cap at decode**: large images are scaled down as they are
    decoded.
18. **Shader bundles revalidated** with the server, so a cached bundle is
    never paired with new code.
19. **An ETC1S KTX2 encoder** in pure Dart, in the asset build. The
    transcoder already exists.
20. **Import and formats.** A large mesh is cut into grid cells at import.
    A scene document keeps data this build does not read through a load
    and save. The environment prefilter is spread over frames instead of
    run all at once.

### A5. Debugging and inspection

21. **More debug channels.** Geometry channels (tangents, UV checker, face
    orientation), a colour per object or material, validation (albedo
    range, non-binary metal, missing tangents), and a debug view set per
    subtree.
22. **A wipe between two debug channels.** Overlays follow the wipe.
23. **Frame capture to JSON and back**, with thumbnails, so a capture can
    be attached to a bug report and opened in the editor.
24. **A memory report** by category, render targets included.
    `releaseTransientTargets` returns the bytes it freed.

### A6. Light, post-processing and the rest

25. **A light's radius and falloff.** Each light has a physical radius
    that widens its highlight, and a falloff exponent that may be 0.
26. **Saving the shadow atlas.** Each light has a caster channel mask. A
    spot's shadow frustum can be narrower than its cone. Mirrors share the
    view's shadow tiles.
27. **Bloom guarded.** A NaN becomes black in the bloom and DoF
    prefilters, and extreme pixels are softly compressed before they
    bloom.
28. **Settings and defaults.** SMAA is tunable at run time, and clustered
    lights are on by default.
29. **A layer for widgets in 3D.** Screens and widgets in the scene draw
    past the tone curve with their colours unchanged, still occluded by
    geometry.
30. **The whole orthographic camera** (fit modes, zoom, pixels per unit,
    negative near, every effect under it), plus the small pieces of the
    pipeline:
    - a linear HDR render texture for mirrors and probes;
    - a draw's instance and index range chosen per pass;
    - one parameter pushed to a group of materials;
    - tolerant custom vertex attributes;
    - a check that a fragment's varyings are written by its vertex stage.

## Stream B: what only a simulation-first engine can do

Each of these is a law from the core shown or heard, not a look painted
over it. Where no measured law exists, it is derived and written up in
Markdown with MathML, as the 0.9 smoke plume was.

### B1. Physics changes how surfaces look

1. **Wet and drying.** Where water or spray has been, a surface darkens
   and turns glossy. It dries at the evaporation rate the heat core
   gives.
2. **Ice and thaw.** A phase change in the heat core: puddles freeze,
   friction drops on ice, and fire melts it.
3. **Soot on walls and ceilings**, deposited from the plume by the fuel's
   soot yield.
4. **Erosion and silting.** The shallow water carries sediment and
   reshapes the ground: channels cut, dams silt up.

### B2. Air and light from physics

5. **One wind field** for bodies, fire, smoke, cloth, foliage, particles
   and sound.
6. **Heat haze** as refraction from the core's temperature field over
   fires and hot surfaces.
7. **A blast wave by its law** (Kinney–Graham overpressure). The front
   travels at its real speed, distorts the picture as it passes, and
   breaks glass past its threshold.
8. **Volumetric smoke**, self-shadowed and lit by its fire, with its
   density from the plume model.

### B3. Seeing and hearing what the simulation knows

9. **A thermal camera** that draws the core's temperatures.
10. **Impact sound from the contact**: modal synthesis by material and
    impulse instead of samples.
11. **Sound through the level**: muffled behind walls by the geometry,
    and under water.
12. **A perception API for AI**: how lit a point is, whether it is seen
    through smoke and dark, and whether a noise is heard. These come from
    the same laws the frame uses.

### B4. Breaking and structures

13. **Fracture by stress.** The core computes the stress in joints, and
    pre-fractured bodies break at their material's strength.
14. **Structures burnt through.** Char thins a beam's section, and a roof
    comes down when its load says so.
15. **Compartments and flooding.** A ship takes water through a breach
    compartment by compartment, lists and sinks by its law.
16. **Ropes and cloth burn and tear** from heat and tension.

### B5. Determinism and replays

17. **Replay to trailer**: a recorded run rendered offline at any size and
    frame rate at the highest quality, with a camera from the editor.
18. **Platforms equal in CI.** A replay's hash is the same on every
    platform and in the browser, and every backend's frames are checked
    against the CPU reference.
19. **Rollback over the whole world.** The rollback session is built on
    the core's snapshots, elements included, not only on the game's own
    state.
20. **A bug report in one file.** A frame capture, the replay tape and
    the snapshot together, opened by the editor on the same frame.

### B6. Tools and access

21. **An agent plays and tests through MCP.** It drives a game, listens to
    the callbacks, and keeps its play as a regression replay.
22. **A physical camera.** Exposure in EV from the physical sky's real
    lux, and a photo mode with aperture, shutter and ISO.
23. **Accessibility in the engine.** Colour-vision modes in the frame,
    high-contrast outlines, and events spoken to screen readers from the
    callbacks. Colour roles and vision simulation exist; these build on
    them.
24. **A reference frame on a server.** The software renderer makes level
    previews and screenshots without a GPU, in CI or on a server.

### B7. Proposed so that each stream holds thirty, for Dmitrii to confirm

25. **Liquids that burn and flow hot.** Oil burning on water as a pool
    fire spreading over the slick, and lava as a viscous hot liquid that
    lights what it touches.
26. **Fire through vegetation**: grass and brush burning across a field by
    a spread law, for strategy and the open world.
27. **Weather into the simulation.** Rain feeds the shallow water and
    wets surfaces, and snow lies and melts.
28. **The sun heats the world.** Solar flux from the physical sky goes
    into the heat core, so ice melts in the sun and dark stone is warm.
29. **Gas leaks and deflagration**: a cloud of fuel gas spreading, and
    burning when its mixture is in range.
30. **Each law a lesson.** Every derivation in `doc/` gets a live
    laboratory in the education packages, with the formula, the slider
    and the frame.

## Stream D: what games need from an engine

1. **Prefabs with nested overrides.** A template object in the editor;
   instances keep their own changes, and a change to the template reaches
   every instance. Big levels and the fifth genre need it.
2. **Occlusion culling.** Two-pass Hi-Z where there is compute (WebGPU,
   wgpu), and portals for rooms everywhere, with hardware occlusion
   queries on WebGL2.
3. **Texture streaming by what is seen.** Only the mips the view needs
   are resident, under a memory budget per device class. Built on KTX2.
4. **Virtual cameras.** Priorities and blends between cameras, framing
   by targets, keeping clear of walls, and shake from an impulse. The
   genres' own cameras (the racing chase camera and the others) move onto
   it.
5. **Adaptive music and mixer snapshots** in `flutter3d_audio`: layers by
   the game's tension, transitions on the beat, stingers, ducking, and
   bus snapshots ("under water", "paused").
6. **Binaural sound (HRTF)** for headphones, from open HRIR sets with
   their sources in `docs/articles`. It goes with the sound through the
   level (B3.11).
7. **Painted terrain**, together with the fifth genre: height and layer
   brushes in the editor, holes for caves, and clipmap LOD. A painted
   ground is one more source for the genre's ground function.
8. **Vegetation.** GPU-instanced grass and trees, a planting brush, grass
   pressed down by bodies, the wind from B2.5, impostors far away, and
   burning from B7.26.
9. **Splines.** Roads, rails, rivers and fences along a spline with the
   mesh bent to it, motion along a spline, and an editor for its points.
   The racing track and River's river move onto it.
10. **Stylised rendering.** Cel ramps, outlines from depth and normals,
    hatching and flat light, through the light and composite hooks of A3
    and a pass for the outlines.
11. **Abilities.** Attributes, timed effects, cooldowns, tags and costs,
    deterministic and in the replay. ISO 13571's burn dose becomes one of
    the effects; the shooter and strategy move onto it.
12. **Effects described as data.** Spawn, force and draw modules for
    particles in a file, as materials are. They can listen to the
    elements' events and collide against depth on the GPU. The editor for
    it comes later.

## Stream E: visualising for teaching, science and industry

13. **Volume rendering.** A DICOM stack or a 3D texture ray-marched with a
    transfer function, maximum-intensity projection, and slices. It shares
    its ray march with the volumetric smoke (B2.8). DICOM is read in pure
    Dart.
14. **Scalar fields on meshes.** Perceptual colour maps (viridis, cividis,
    diverging ones) with a legend and scale, and contour lines. The
    thermal camera (B3.9) and stresses (B4.13) use them.
15. **Sections and isosurfaces.** Clipping planes with the cut capped,
    clipping by a box, and marching cubes over a scalar field.
16. **Vector fields.** Streamlines, arrow glyphs, line-integral
    convolution and tracer particles, first for the core's own fields:
    the shallow water's flow, the wind, the plume.
17. **Point clouds.** LAS, LAZ, E57 and PLY in an octree streamed by
    view, eye-dome lighting, and point size by distance. It goes with the
    texture streaming (D3) and the floating origin of the fifth genre.
18. **Geographic coordinates.** WGS84, and double precision in the scene
    by drawing relative to the eye. It is the base for the fifth genre and
    for placing point clouds. Tiled city streaming comes later.
19. **Robotics.** URDF imported into the core's joints, actuators with PID,
    inverse kinematics for a manipulator, and sensors (lidar, depth
    camera). Deterministic physics and replays are what teaching
    simulators lack.
20. **Live data on the scene.** MQTT and WebSocket values bound to nodes:
    colour by value, alarm thresholds, and the history recorded and
    played back through our replays. It works in the browser too.
21. **Measuring and annotating.** Distances, angles and areas snapped to
    vertices and edges. Labels with leader lines pinned to points of the
    model, as Flutter widgets over the scene that hide when occluded.
    Guided tours from label to label.
22. **Exploded views and assembly.** Parts moved apart along the assembly
    hierarchy, assembly and disassembly steps animated, and one part shown
    with the rest made translucent.
23. **A reference path tracer**, on the CPU and in WebGPU compute. It
    renders the same materials and lights progressively, for stills and
    for the trailer (B5.17), and is the reference the raster path is
    checked against.
24. **Molecules, as balls and sticks.** PDB read into impostor spheres and
    cylinders.
25. **3D plots.** Surfaces z = f(x, y), scatter plots and curves in space,
    and axes with ticks, labels and a legend, as a widget for Flutter apps
    and for the laboratories (B7.30).
26. **Results of other simulations.** VTK legacy, VTU and CSV time
    series, scrubbed in time, in the viewer and in the browser.
27. **WebXR.** A model placed in the room from the browser, with a shadow
    on the real floor. Stereo exists already.

## Order inside the streams

- **A.** Starts with A1 and A2, which change every frame and its goldens.
  A3 follows, because B1, B2 and C need the vertex block and scene depth.
- **B.** Starts with what reuses 0.9's core: B1.1, B1.3, B2.5 and B3.12.
  The C-core items (B1.2, B1.4, B4) need ABI and snapshot bumps, so they
  are batched to bump once.
- **C.** Its first step does not wait for A or B.
