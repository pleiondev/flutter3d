# A fifth genre: exploring an open world (0.10)

Agreed with Dmitrii on 2026-10-08: worth doing, in 0.10 rather than 0.9, whose
scope is closed to the cheap game extensions (decision 1 of
`0.9-release.md`).

## Why it is a genre and not an idea

Four demos are already built on this genre without a package to share. Each
writes its own copy:

- **The ground is a function of where you stand.** Hollow (`terrain.dart`),
  Reef (`terrain.dart`) and Water (`valley.dart`) each read the liquids'
  grid, the collision mesh and the drawn mesh off one height function, so
  the three cannot disagree. Each world is a 64 m square, with its own
  constants for grid, cell and drawn resolution.
- **Someone who moves through it in more than one way.** Hollow's
  `StoneCar` is the core's vehicle pushed by whoever sits in it. Reef's
  `Diver` is a body held up by what it displaces and trimmed by a jacket.
  Getting in and out, and switching bodies, is written per game.
- **Things to find.** Reef's `Find` (a thing on the bottom brought up by a
  lifting bag) and Hollow's building site waiting for stone. Each is a
  place, a condition and a "done".
- **The world as you left it.** The sandbox saves its voxel changes
  (`flutter3d_voxel`) and restores them.
- **A day that passes.** The sandbox's `Daylight` moves the sun through
  `PhysicalSky` and gives a play-tuned moon.

## What `flutter3d_game_explore` would hold

The first step takes what is already written three times:

1. **Ground as a function.** One `GroundFunction` (height and surface at
   a point) from which the package builds the liquids' heightfield, the
   collision heightfield and the drawn mesh at a resolution per platform
   (phone half as fine, as Reef does today). Hollow, Reef and Water move
   onto it.
2. **An avatar that changes bodies.** On foot ↔ vehicle ↔ swimming/diving:
   who the camera follows, which input map is live, where the body is put
   back when it steps out. Hollow's car and Reef's diver are the first two
   bodies.
3. **Finds and places.** A place, what makes it done, and an event when it
   is. Reef's finds and Hollow's site move onto it. This goes through the
   run, so replays hold.
4. **The day.** `Daylight` moves from the sandbox into the package as it
   stands.

The second step is what makes the world open:

5. **Chunks streamed around the player.** The ground function is cut into
   chunks loaded and dropped around the avatar. Drawn meshes and collision
   follow it first.
6. **A floating origin.** At float32 a position a few kilometres out has
   centimetre steps, and a shadow or a contact shows it. The world is
   re-centred under the avatar, with every body, emitter and light moved
   with it.
7. **The world as you left it, for any ground.** A save as a difference
   from the generated world. The sandbox already does this for voxels.

## The risk

The core's shallow water and fires live in a world of fixed size. A
simulation region that travels with the player has to bring its water,
heat and bodies with it and stay deterministic, so that a replay still
replays. That is work in the C core (`f3d_shallow.c`, `f3d_heat.c`,
snapshots), not wiring in Dart, and it is the part to estimate first.
Until then the first step stands on its own: four demos sharing one
package, each world still 64 m.

## Done when

- Hollow, Reef, Water and the sandbox depend on `flutter3d_game_explore`,
  and their own copies of the ground, the avatar, finds and the day are
  gone.
- A test that the drawn, collision and liquid grids agree at every
  vertex they share.
- A test for every body change (in and out), for a find completing
  through a replay, and, in the second step, for the floating origin
  (a body 10 km out moves and collides as it does at the origin).
- The package's README and ARCHITECTURE.md entry, and the genre counted
  on the site.
